A redline of frappe/erpnext #42807 · open since 17 Aug 2024

The 45 days is a ceiling, not the rule

ERPNext has no way to run a payment term from the date goods were received, which is what India's MSMED Act requires. Issue #42807 asks for one. The feature is right and the specification is wrong in three places — each of which would ship an implementation that quietly under-reports a buyer's statutory exposure.

Three corrections to the specification

Correction 1 · the period

Payments to MSME suppliers must be made within 45 days 45 days where the terms are in writing, and 15 days where they are not.

MSMED Act 2006, s.15. Forty-five days is the maximum a written agreement may stipulate, not a default that applies to every supply. Where there is no written agreement the Act gives 15 days, and no contract can extend it.

An implementation that hardcodes 45 days reports a due date up to 30 days late for every purchase made on an informal arrangement — which is most small purchases.

Correction 2 · who is covered

Add a checkbox in the Supplier master to mark a supplier as MSME micro or small, as classified under S.O. 1364(E).

MSMED Act 2006, s.2(n). A “supplier” is a micro or small enterprise that has filed a Udyam memorandum. Medium enterprises are excluded entirely — the M in MSME is not covered by the delayed-payment provisions, and s.43B(h) does not reach them either.

A single “is MSME” checkbox applies the 45-day clock to medium suppliers who are entitled to no such thing, voiding valid contractual terms and raising compliance alarms that do not exist.

Correction 3 · where the clock starts

Due Date = Receipt Date the day of acceptance + the allowed period.

MSMED Act 2006, s.2(b), explanation (i). Acceptance is the day of actual delivery — unless the buyer objects in writing within 15 days, in which case the clock starts only when the supplier removes the objection. Receipt is the usual case, not the rule.

Treating receipt as always decisive starts the clock too early on disputed deliveries, and overstates interest the supplier cannot actually claim.

What the patch changes in core

The statutory logic does not belong in ERPNext core — erpnext/regional/ has no India directory, because India compliance lives in the separate india_compliance app. What core is genuinely missing is the generic hook: a payment term that runs from receipt rather than from the invoice. That is not an Indian idea. The EU Late Payment Directive 2011/7/EU runs from receipt of the goods too. 

  def get_due_date(term, posting_date=None, bill_date=None, receipt_date=None):

+   elif term.due_date_based_on == "Day(s) after receipt date":
+       # Several jurisdictions run the payment clock from receipt of the goods
+       # rather than the invoice date -- India's MSMED Act s.15 and the EU Late
+       # Payment Directive 2011/7/EU among them. Fall back to the invoice date
+       # when nothing has been received yet, so this term can never produce a
+       # null due date on an invoice raised ahead of delivery.
+       due_date = add_days(receipt_date or date, cint(term.credit_days))
  • Adds Day(s) after receipt date to due_date_based_on on Payment Term, Payment Terms Template Detail and Payment Schedule — and deliberately not to discount_validity_based_on, which stays invoice-based.
  • Resolves the receipt date from the submitted Purchase Receipts behind the invoice, taking the earliest, so a buyer cannot stretch the clock by invoicing late.
  • Falls back to the invoice date when nothing has been received, so the term can never yield a null due date.
  • Two tests in ERPNext's own style, plus the statutory module and conformance vectors kept outside core.

How this was verified

  • 20 tests on the reference implementation, in plain Python with no framework — so an accountant can read it and a maintainer can run it without a bench.
  • 11 tests executing ERPNext's real patched get_due_date against a stubbed frappe. Four of them pin the three pre-existing options so the change cannot regress them.
  • 288 conformance vectors generated from the Python module and asserted against the JavaScript port that drives this page, so the page cannot drift from the module.
  • The patch is confirmed to git apply cleanly against 1f83906 on develop.

What has not been done: ERPNext's own suite needs bench, MariaDB and Redis, none of which were available. The two tests added to test_payment_terms_template.py follow the file's existing DB-free pattern but have not been run in a live instance.

Sources. MSMED Act 2006 ss.2(b), 2(n), 15 and 16. Income Tax Act 1961 s.43B(h), inserted by the Finance Act 2023 and effective from AY 2024–25, which reaches micro and small enterprises only and excludes traders. Classification per notification S.O. 1364(E) of 21 March 2025, in force 1 April 2025: micro up to ₹2.5cr investment and ₹10cr turnover, small up to ₹25cr and ₹100cr, medium up to ₹125cr and ₹500cr, on composite criteria.
